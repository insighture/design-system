Executive summary

Faster releases, fewer failures

In the twelve months since the move to the new platform, the team ships nearly twice as often and fixes problems faster. The gains are real but uneven, and two areas need attention before the next phase.

47%shorter lead time for changes
3.2×more frequent deployments
−38%change failure rate

What changed

Deployment moved from a weekly, manually approved release train to on-demand releases through an automated pipeline. Environments are now created from code, and every change runs the same checks.

  • Lead time for changes fell from 14.2 to 7.5 days.
  • Deployments rose from 4 to 13 a month.
  • Rollbacks now take minutes instead of hours.

Key takeaway

The platform has paid for itself in delivery speed. The remaining risk is in testing, not tooling.

Where it fell short

Automated test coverage grew more slowly than release frequency, so a larger share of defects now reach production before they are caught. Two legacy services were never moved and still need the old release process.

“The pipeline changed how we work. We stopped batching changes because we were afraid of them.”Engineering lead, platform team

Recommendations

  1. Raise automated test coverage on the five highest-change services to 80% by Q1.
  2. Retire or migrate the two remaining legacy services.
  3. Pilot AI-assisted code review on one team and measure the defect escape rate.
